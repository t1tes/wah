Letter 7: 777

Dear Unicornsiren99, You are so weird, and I mean that in the best way possible. I think one of my favorite things about you is that you never really tried to make yourself normal. Even when you probably wondered if you were too much, too strange, or too different, there was always something about you that refused to completely hide. And honestly, I love that. I love your weird love for bugs. I still think it's funny how you can look at something that most people would immediately avoid and somehow find something interesting about it, the way you can be fascinated by the smallest things, and the way you can look at something everyone else ignores and think, "Wait, isn't this actually kind of cool?" I love your weird food combinations too. Sometimes I genuinely question what is happening in your head when you put certain things together, but then again, that's very Jayme of you. You have your own logic for everything, and somehow it works. I love your random noises, I love when your mood changes out of nowhere, I love when you're on your phone too much and I pretend to judge you for it, and I love the moments where you gag after eating something and somehow it becomes one of the funniest things ever. I love all these little things that you probably don't even think about, because those are the things that make you feel real. People always talk about loving someone's big qualities, but I think the little things are what make someone impossible to replace. It's the things nobody else would notice, the habits, the jokes, the random expressions, and the strange little parts of someone that only become familiar because you spent enough time with them. And I hope you never lose those parts of yourself, especially the parts you once thought you had to hide or questioned. I know your identity hasn't always been the easiest thing to understand or accept. I know there were times where you were scared of what it meant, scared of how people would see you, and scared of what it would change. That's when you started wondering if "normal" is only the world your own beliefs have made familiar. People spend so much time judging each other's decisions without ever seeing the life that made those decisions feel obvious. Maybe that's why you've stopped wanting people to tell you exactly what's right and wrong. Not because you think every decision is wise, but because some lessons only become yours after you've lived them. Look at how far you've come. Think about the version of yourself who was still trying to figure everything out, the version of you who was scared of a future you couldn't even imagine yet. She made it here. She found people who love her. She found pieces of herself that she didn't know existed. And I hope one day you can look back at her with kindness, not frustration, not regret, and not wondering why it took so long, because you were never late, you were just becoming yourself at your own pace. I always think about this idea that sometimes we spend so much time fearing a tragedy that has already happened. We keep waiting for something terrible to arrive, but we don't realize we already survived the thing we were afraid of. And maybe that's what happened: maybe you spent so much time preparing for a life where you would never be understood, only to slowly find people who saw you exactly as you are; maybe you spent so much time wondering if you would ever be comfortable in your own skin, only to eventually realize you were already on your way there. I know there are still things you struggle with, and I know becoming yourself isn't something that happens once and then suddenly everything is easy. But I hope you give yourself credit for every step you took, for every moment you chose honesty over fear, and for every time you allowed yourself to exist a little more freely. I think of queerness as this long thread of hurt that people before us have carried, and I always wonder what we choose to do with that thread. When I think about you, I think you'll make something beautiful out of it. Not because you have to turn every difficult thing into something meaningful, or because your pain is what makes you special, but because you are someone who has always created things from what you're given. You collect pieces: songs, memories, random thoughts, little moments, and somehow you make something out of them. So maybe you'll take every thread, every part of yourself that you once questioned, and weave it into something that feels completely yours, something you can look at and say: This is me. Not the version people expected, not the version you were afraid to become, just you; the weird, wonderful, bug-loving, music-loving, random-food-combination-making Jayme that I know. And I hope you never apologize for being her, because honestly, I wouldn't know how to love anyone else.
