Letter 13: Past

Dear Madi? Still can't get used to calling you that, honestly. It's funny how a name can feel so strange when you've known someone by another one for so long, like somehow a different name means a different chapter, even though you're still the same person underneath it. Tell me that you think I'm pretty, and I'll probably react way better. I'm joking... kind of. But I think there's something interesting about the way we see ourselves compared to how other people see us. Sometimes we spend so much time searching for proof that we are not enough that we forget to notice the people who already see us differently. I think about how falling in love with someone is not always about discovering a person who completes you. Maybe it's about meeting someone who reminds you of a part of yourself you forgot existed, someone who brings out something that was already there, a version of you that was always waiting. And I think that's what you did: you reminded me of things I forgot I could feel, you made ordinary moments feel important, and you made me want to be more honest, more open, and more myself. I hope you know that the things you've done, the mistakes you've made, and the moments you wish you could change never became the only thing I saw when I looked at you. I know you carry things: things you regret, things you wish you handled differently, and things you wish you could go back and rewrite. But I hope you don't let those moments convince you that they are the entire story, because they aren't. A person is not only the things they regret; a person is also everything they tried to do right, everything they cared about, everything they loved, and everything they were brave enough to feel. And I always think about this: it cannot be a mistake to have cared, it cannot be selfish to have kept something meaningful close to you, it cannot be an error to have tried, and it cannot be incorrect to have loved. Even if something ends badly, that does not erase the fact that it once mattered. I think sometimes we judge ourselves because we know how things ended, looking back with information we didn't have before and blaming ourselves for not making different choices. But we were making decisions with the version of ourselves that existed at the time, doing what we thought we could do. Maybe that doesn't excuse everything, and maybe there are things we wish we handled differently, but I don't think regret should erase every good thing that existed before it. You are what you love, not what loves you. I think that line stays with me because it reminds me that the things we give our hearts to become part of who we are: the music, the people, the memories, the places, and the little things that shaped us. They all leave something behind. I know there were moments where you woke up and wondered if you even had a future, moments where everything felt empty, where you felt disconnected from yourself, and where even getting through the day felt like something you had to convince yourself to do. I wish I could go back and sit beside that version of you, not to tell her everything would magically become easy or pretend I know exactly what she needed, but just to remind her that she would meet people she never expected, experience things she never imagined, and become someone who could look back and realize she survived days she thought she wouldn't. I think there's an art to everything: even leaving, even letting go, and even accepting that some things cannot return to how they were. Sometimes we think moving forward means forgetting, but I don't think that's true; sometimes moving forward means carrying the memory without letting it control where you go. Your commitment to your future has to become stronger than your attachment to your past. Not because the past didn't matter (because it did, which is exactly why it's so hard to leave), but you cannot spend your entire life standing at a door that already closed. You deserve to see what happens when you walk through the next one. I know there are moments where people try to escape themselves, moments where they search for anything that makes them feel different, even if only temporarily, a different feeling, a different version of themselves, or anything that makes the noise quiet for a while. But eventually, you always come back to yourself. And I hope when you do, you're kinder to the person waiting there, because she has been through everything with you: every mistake, every heartbreak, every good memory, and every version of you that you had to leave behind. She stayed. So maybe the next chapter is not about becoming someone completely new, but maybe it's about finally meeting the person you've been carrying all along: the person who was always there, the person who was always worth knowing, the person I knew: you.
