Letter 12: Homesick

Dear Jayme, Rest in peace, 206 Jayme Cave. Long live the VI American flag. Long live the research papers, the IKEA stuffed toys, and the random things that somehow made that place feel like ours. It's strange thinking about leaving a place behind. A room can just be a room to everyone else, but when you spend enough time somewhere, it starts carrying pieces of you: the little routines, the conversations, the nights where you stayed up too late, and the things that only made sense because they happened there. I think that's why saying goodbye feels so strange, because you're not just leaving a place; you're leaving a version of yourself that existed there. And Jayme, you're leaving, and that's okay. I'm totally not sobbing on my way back; I totally am. I think a part of me always knew this day would come, but knowing something is going to happen doesn't make it easier when it actually does. There's something strange about watching someone you care about move toward something new, because you're happy for them and want them to experience everything waiting for them, but there's also this quiet sadness because you know things won't stay exactly the same. I remember this quote about how when you gain something in life, your real job is to use it to help someone else; that if you have freedom, you help someone else find theirs, and if you have power, you use it to empower someone else. And I think that's something I've always seen in you. You have this ability to make people feel less alone, even when you don't realize you're doing it. Sometimes just being yourself gives someone else permission to be themselves too. In this terrifying world, you have the ability to offer someone a little relief, and I hope you never forget how much a little relief can mean, because sometimes a little is actually a lot. Advice can save you from pain, but it can also save you from discovering something you could never have learned any other way. You don't want someone else to live your life correctly for you. You want to experience it honestly enough that whatever you become actually belongs to you. Maybe that's why it's so difficult to judge another person's path. Everyone is trying to solve different questions with different lives. Your journey was never meant to look like anyone else's. It was only ever meant to become your own. I think about birds before migration, how there's this strange feeling before they leave, this restlessness, this fear, and this knowing that something has to change even if staying would be easier. Maybe humans are like that too. Maybe leaving is scary because part of us wants to stay where we already know who we are. I hope you don't let familiarity become the reason you stop moving, and I hope you never stay somewhere just because it's comfortable. Even if one day you grow to love the Philippines, even if you find people there who become important to you, and even if leaving becomes harder because there is suddenly more to lose, I hope you still remember the person you are trying to become, the places you want to see, the things you want to experience, and the life you imagined before you knew what was comfortable. Because sometimes the direction we least want to take is the one that changes us the most. I know hope can be exhausting. Sometimes people talk about hope like it's this easy, beautiful thing, but sometimes hope is actually terrifying. Sometimes hoping means admitting you want something, and wanting something means giving yourself the chance to be disappointed. But I hope you keep hoping anyway, and I hope you keep moving, because if you keep waiting for the perfect moment, you'll spend forever waiting. There is never going to be a day where everything is perfectly lined up; sometimes you just have to go, and sometimes you have to take the step even while you're scared. Look back at the places that made you homesick and the places that made you who you are, but don't let them become the only place you live. Because you can travel across continents, cross oceans, and experience completely different worlds, but you will always bring yourself with you: your thoughts, your memories, your fears, your dreams, and your heart. And maybe that's not something to escape from; maybe it's something to learn how to live with. Your own presence is something you will always have, so I hope you learn to enjoy your own company. I hope you become someone who can sit with herself and not immediately search for something else to fill the silence. I hope you stay open: open to new experiences, open to meeting people, open to changing your mind, and open to becoming someone you haven't even imagined yet. And if it takes time, let it take time. Not everything has to happen immediately, some things are worth waiting for, and some versions of yourself take a while to arrive. So goodbye, 206 Jayme Cave. Goodbye to the little pieces of life that existed there, but hello to everything waiting for you next. I hope wherever you go, you remember that you are allowed to leave, you are allowed to grow, and you are allowed to become someone new.
