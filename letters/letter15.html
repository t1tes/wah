Future

Dear Jayme, A tattoo in China, a tattoo in Switzerland, a tattoo in Georgia... I still think it's funny how random our conversations could become. How we could go from something completely ordinary to imagining places we've never even been, talking about things we might do years from now like the future was something we could already touch. Maybe that's one of the things I loved about us: the way we dreamed, and the way we could imagine a life beyond whatever was happening in the present. I hope the world treats you better, I really do. I hope people learn to see the difference between your kindness and naivety, because I know sometimes the world has a way of making people regret having soft hearts. People can make you feel like caring too much is something to be ashamed of, or like being hopeful means you don't understand how things work. But I hope you never let that completely disappear from you. I hope you stay someone who gets excited, someone who believes things can be different, and someone who still finds reasons to care. Because that part of you is not something you need to lose just because the world is sometimes cruel. I think about this image of warmth: of finding a place that feels like a campfire, a place where you can finally stop being cold, a place where you feel safe enough to put your hands near the flame and believe it will not burn you. I hope you find places like that, people like that, and moments where you don't have to constantly protect yourself. I hope you find somewhere that feels like home, not because you have to stay there forever, but because you were allowed to exist there fully. I think about how strange it is that someone can become part of the way you imagine your future, not because you planned it or forced it, but because it just happens. Suddenly there are places you picture with them, things you want to show them, and days you want to spend together. And maybe that's why it hurts when the future changes, because you're not only losing what happened, but also losing the things you imagined, the ordinary days that never got the chance to exist. If I could give you anything, I don't think it would be flowers. Not because flowers aren't beautiful (they are), but flowers eventually wilt. Promises can also change, and words can become memories. So if I could give you anything, it would be every ordinary day I have: every boring morning, every random conversation, and every moment where nothing special is happening but we're still there. Because I think those are the moments that matter most, not the perfect ones, but the normal ones where you wake up and realize someone is still beside you. You told me before that you weren't really afraid of death, that you had thought about what you wanted to happen after: donating your body to science, which I still think is a little cruel, by the way, or being cremated. And I remember thinking about how strange it is that humans spend so much time wondering what happens after we're gone, while also trying so hard to make meaning out of the time we have here. Maybe that's what love does: it reminds us that we are temporary, and somehow makes us want to stay anyway. If all that remains of us one day is ash, then I hope the world was kind to you while you were here. I hope you saw beautiful things, I hope you laughed until your stomach hurt, and I hope you loved things without being afraid of losing them. I hope you never let the possibility of an ending stop you from experiencing the middle, because endings are unavoidable, but that doesn't make the moments before them meaningless; they are still worth having, worth remembering, and worth loving. And if there was ever a version of the future where we got to experience all those things we talked about, I think I would have loved that: the tattoos, the trips, the random adventures, and the ordinary days. But even without knowing what happens next, I'm grateful that I got to imagine them with you. Because for a while, when I thought about the future, you were there, and that meant something.
